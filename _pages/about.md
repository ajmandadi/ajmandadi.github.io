I'm a 4th-year PhD student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Georgia Tech, advised by Dr. Tom Conte. I work on emerging and novel computer architectures.

My research broadly focuses on two directions:

- **High-precision analog accelerators:** Developing architectures that bridge the gap between the energy efficiency of analog computing and the stringent numerical precision requirements of scientific computing. We leverage the Residue Number System (RNS) to enable high-precision computation on low-precision analog hardware, while developing error mitigation techniques to address analog non-idealities and ensuring numerical accuracy/reliable convergence in iterative scientific solvers.
  - Awarded [CRNCH Fellowship-2026](https://www.cc.gatech.edu/news/crnch-fellowship-awards-computing-innovation) for innovative research.
- **Hardware-Software Co-design for irregular workloads:** Exploring fine-grained asynchronous execution models (FA-BSP) to enhance scalability for irregular workloads such as sparse linear algebra and graph analytics. We investigate hardware support for asynchronous task execution and moving compute closer to data through co-design that improves performance and resource utilization in HPC systems.
